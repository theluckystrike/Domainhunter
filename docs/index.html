The .ORG Thesis (and Why It Died)

The Hypothesis

.org domains have a 1.4% catch rate (vs 7.6% for .com). 90% go uncaught. We can snipe high-authority .org at $6.99 via Dynadot general availability after DropCatch's dropzone closes.

The Test

I scanned 79,896 .org domains in the Majestic Million, cross-referenced against the pendingDelete feed, and found exactly 1 match in the top 100K: nucleuscms.org at Majestic rank 34,752, Ahrefs DR 69, Wikipedia + StackOverflow backlinks.

I stacked dual-platform backorders: Dynadot $24.99 + DropCatch $15/$59. The domain dropped June 1 at 15:15 UTC. DropCatch caught it instantly. 12 bidders. $550+ and climbing.

The "90% uncaught" stat is survivorship bias. 90% of .org domains go uncaught because 90% have no authority. The 10% that get caught ARE the 10% worth catching. DropCatch catches .org via PIR Dropzone using the exact same infrastructure as .com. Same bidders, same prices, zero edge.

Gap 1 Sniper: Works But Useless

I built a sniper that fires at 14:34 UTC after DropCatch's dropzone closes. It caught koajs.org (35.7K GitHub stars, 7.4M npm/week) and fabricjs.org (27K stars) at $6.99 each.

Problem #1: Both were active projects — must delete to avoid UDRP.

Problem #2: The sniper only catches domains DropCatch didn't bother catching. What DropCatch leaves behind = domains with no real authority. The gap exists but the gap is filled with junk.

7 Lessons From 54 Sprints

#1 Drop-catching is a whale game

The auction market is efficient. DR 50+ domains attract 8-12 professional bidders and sell for $500-$2,000+. You need $500-$5,000 per domain to compete. If your budget is under $500/domain, you cannot win authority drops. Period.

#2 .org = .com at auction

Zero difference. DropCatch catches .org via PIR Dropzone with the same infrastructure. I tested this with the best .org find in 54 sprints (nucleuscms.org, DR 69, Wikipedia backlinks). 12 bidders, $550+. Identical dynamics to .com.

#3 Finding domains is worthless — acquiring them is the business

My pipeline found every valuable domain. It identified nucleuscms.org as Majestic rank 34,752 a week before the drop. It verified DR 69 via Ahrefs. It stacked dual-platform backorders. Finding is the easy part. Winning the auction is the only part that matters.

#4 The "90% uncaught" stat is survivorship bias

90% of .org domains go uncaught because 90% have no authority. The 10% that get caught are the 10% worth catching. The only uncaught domains with "value" were active open-source projects (koajs.org, fabricjs.org) that you'd have to delete to avoid UDRP.

#5 GitHub stars ≠ domain value

23 .org domains with 5K-53K GitHub stars sit available at $6.99 right now (normalizecss.org, semanticui.org, materializecss.org...). Nobody wants them because GitHub stars don't create backlinks. Majestic/Ahrefs authority is the only metric that correlates with auction price.

#6 Same 5 bidders win everything

seodomains, first1, obotceo, and a few anonymous account IDs appear in every authority domain auction on DropCatch. They run automated bidding with $500-$5,000+ ceilings. This is their full-time business.

#7 Build, don't flip

The only domain generating potential value is dismissticket.com — a content site targeting a $3.75B market at $8.48 CPC. Building a site creates value. Holding parked domains waiting for buyers does not. 5,556 total Afternic impressions across two listed domains, zero inquiries. The flip market for small portfolios is dead.

The Math That Killed It

To make drop-catching profitable at small scale, you need ALL of these simultaneously:

Acquisition budget: $500-$2,000 per domain (minimum to compete at DropCatch auctions)

Win rate: ~20-30% of auctions entered (you will be outbid on most)

Flip markup: 2-5x acquisition price (to cover losses + renewal costs)

Time to flip: 3-12 months (domain sales are slow)

Portfolio size: 10-20 domains minimum for statistical coverage

Total capital required: $5,000-$20,000 liquid, deployed over 6-12 months

My budget was $213 total with $59-$250 max bids. This was never going to work. The data proved it 8 times.

What To Do Instead

Build content sites on domains you already own. One page ranking for a $8+ CPC keyword generates more revenue than 100 parked domains. Build creates compounding value. Flipping creates one-time exits that require winning auctions against whales.

The scanning pipeline, the automation, the API clients — all reusable for other projects. 1,297 tests and clean architecture. It's just pointed at the wrong market.

If you're thinking about domain drop-catching with under $5K: don't. Build something instead.
