Full-service traffic ticket law firm. 40 offices across Florida, Georgia, and California. Flat fees starting at $49. Over 5 million cases defended since 1987. Monetizes through direct legal services only. Limited to 3 states. No DIY option. No educational content beyond basic blog posts.

Comedy-based traffic school courses. DMV-approved in most states. $20 to $60 per course. 30+ year brand. No ticket dismissal service. Content is course marketing, not educational.

Online traffic school and defensive driving courses. State-approved in nearly every state. $6 to $90 depending on state. Affiliate program pays 20 to 40 percent commission with 90-day cookie. Strong resource library but generic driving advice, not ticket-specific.

USER INTENT RESEARCH
---------------------

People searching for traffic ticket help are overwhelmingly in a panic-driven, time-sensitive, high-stakes emotional state. They have just received a ticket, often their first, feel anxious, and need immediate clarity on what to do.


THE NUMBER ONE THING USERS WANT

"What do I do now?"

The dominant search intent is immediate, panicked guidance. Users are not casually browsing. They have a ticket in hand and a deadline ticking.

Primary needs, ranked by urgency

1. Avoid insurance increase. A single speeding ticket raises insurance by roughly 24 percent ($500+ per year for 3 to 5 years). The insurance hit is the real cost, not the fine.

2. Avoid points on license. Points trigger insurance increases and can lead to license suspension.

3. Know all options before the deadline. The single biggest regret users express is paying the ticket too quickly without knowing alternatives. Paying equals a guilty plea.

4. Save money on the fine itself. Paradoxically, this is the least important factor despite being the most obvious one.


WHAT INFORMATION IS HARDEST TO FIND

State-specific rules are the number one gap. Every state has different rules for point systems, traffic school eligibility, deadlines, and out-of-state handling. No single site provides clean, queryable, state-specific answers.

The true total cost of a ticket is poorly documented. Users want to know the combined fine plus court fees plus traffic school cost plus insurance increase over 3 to 5 years. A few calculators exist but none are comprehensive.

Court procedures terrify users. Common questions from forums include "What happens when I walk in?" and "What do I say to the judge?" and "What if the officer doesn't show up?"

Decision frameworks are missing. Users want a clear yes or no on "should I fight this?" but no existing resource gives a clean decision tree.


WHAT FRUSTRATIONS DO USERS EXPRESS

Every answer starts with "it depends on your state" and users are exhausted by generic advice.

Most users did not know that paying the fine is an admission of guilt.

Court websites are notoriously outdated and confusing.

Lawyer sites answer questions with "contact us for a free consultation" instead of giving actual information.

Traffic ticket dismissal rules for the top 10 states by volume.


| State          | Traffic School | Frequency        | Online | Hours | Fine Status        | Point System     |
|----------------|----------------|------------------|--------|-------|--------------------|------------------|

KEY STATE DIFFERENCES

California "masks" the ticket from the public DMV record but does not truly dismiss it. Insurance companies cannot see a masked ticket.

Texas requires formal court approval before enrolling in defensive driving. You must plead guilty or no contest and pay court fees first.

Florida has the strictest lifetime cap at 5 total traffic school elections. You must notify the Clerk of Court within 10 days.

New York does NOT offer traffic school dismissal. The PIRP only reduces points and provides a 10 percent insurance discount for 3 years. True dismissal requires local Traffic Diversion Programs.

Georgia does NOT accept self-paced online courses. Only live instructor-led classes via Zoom or in-person.

Illinois uses a unique Court Supervision system. Rather than simple traffic school, it is a probationary sentence. If you stay clean during supervision, the entire conviction is dismissed.

Virginia uses a unique positive/negative point system. You earn +1 safe driving point per clean year (max +5). The 8-hour course awards +5 points instantly, equivalent to 5 years of clean driving.

(function() {
  "use strict";

  var MAX_STEPS = 6;
  var data = { state: "", violation: "", firstOffense: true };

  function nextStep(step) {
    if (step < 1 || step > MAX_STEPS) return;
    var current = document.querySelector(".quiz-step.active");
    if (current) current.classList.remove("active");
    var next = document.getElementById("quiz-step-" + step);
    if (next) next.classList.add("active");
    if (step === MAX_STEPS) generateResult();

  function setFirstOffense(v) { data.firstOffense = v; }

    var el = document.getElementById("recommendation");

    var trafficSchoolStates = ["CA","TX","FL","OH","VA","IL"];
    var canDoSchool = data.firstOffense &&
                      trafficSchoolStates.indexOf(data.state) !== -1;

        "This prevents points on your record and avoids insurance increases.";

        "Based on your situation, contesting the ticket gives you " +
        "the best chance of avoiding points and insurance increases.";
    }

  window.setViolation = setViolation;
  window.setFirstOffense = setFirstOffense;
