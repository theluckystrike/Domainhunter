GoDaddy's 4 Marketplace Systems — The Critical Distinction

Sprint 10 confused these 4 completely different systems. Understanding them is essential.

1. STANDARD REGISTRATION — $2–$20/yr
URL: godaddy.com/domainsearch
What: Fresh unregistered domains at retail price
How: Search → Register → Done
Price: $2–$20/yr for standard TLDs
Identifier: WHOIS shows "No match" / Available
2. AFTERMARKET/PREMIUM (AFTERNIC) — $50–$1M+
URL: afternic.com / surfaced in godaddy.com search
What: Owner-set prices through Afternic (GoDaddy subsidiary)
THIS IS WHAT SPRINT 10 FOUND
Price: $50 to $1,000,000+ (set by domain speculator)
Identifier: Afternic API sellable:true, ns1.afternic.com NS
Commission: 15–25%
3. EXPIRED AUCTIONS — $1 starting bid
URL: auctions.godaddy.com (requires $4.99/yr membership)
What: GoDaddy-registered domains that expired. 10-day auction.
Price: $1 starting bid, market-driven
Volume: ~62,463 domains ending per day
Identifier: clientRenewProhibited EPP, LANDER_SYSTEM=PW
Risk: Owner can still renew during auction (full refund to bidder)
4. CLOSEOUT — $5–$11 BIN
URL: auctions.godaddy.com → Closeout filter
What: Unsold auction domains. Buy Now, price drops daily over 5 days.
Volume: ~256,810 available at any time
Schedule: Day 1: $11 → Day 2: $10 → Day 3: $8 → Day 4: $6 → Day 5: $5
After Day 5: Released back to registry for standard registration
Key: 99%+ are low quality. Good names grabbed at $11 on Day 1.

THE TRAP: How Search Results Fool You

When you search for a domain on godaddy.com and see a price like $1,540 or $2,999, that is an AFTERMARKET PREMIUM price set by the current owner through Afternic.

It is NOT a closeout price. Real closeout prices are $5–$11 and are only visible through the GoDaddy Auctions interface or inventory files.

RULE: Never trust a price from GoDaddy domain search results. Always verify the marketplace type using Afternic API (afternic.com/forsale/{domain}), EPP status codes, and nameserver analysis.

Domain Lifecycle Timeline

Day 0
Registration expires. Owner can still renew normally during auto-renew grace period (0-42 days).
Day 26
Expired Auction begins. 10-day bidding period. Starting bid $1. Owner can still renew (cancels auction).
Day 36
Closeout begins (if auction got zero bids). Price: $11→$10→$8→$6→$5 over 5 days.
Day 42
Released to registry. Domain enters PendingDelete. Available for standard registration in ~5 days.
Day 47+
Public drop. Drop-catch services (DropCatch, SnapNames, NameJet) compete to register first.

Sprint 12 Lessons Learned

Lesson 1: GoDaddy Has 4 Marketplaces That Are Trivially Confused

Sprint 10 recommended buying rbiwoodtools.com ($5) and shrimpvietnam.com ($5) as "GoDaddy Closeouts." Actual checkout prices: $1,540 and $2,075 respectively — these were Afternic/GoDaddy Premium aftermarket listings.

Financial impact: Prevented $3,605 in overspending. Would have blown the entire $600 budget 6x over.

Rule: Always verify price at checkout. Use Afternic API (afternic.com/forsale/{domain}) to check if a domain is an aftermarket premium listing before recommending purchase.

Lesson 2: Expired Domains in Grace Period Can Be Renewed

aidevtools.com was expired with clientHold + renewPeriod status in Sprint 10. By Sprint 12, the owner renewed it (expiry extended to 2027-05-06). NameSilo has a 30-day grace period.

Financial impact: Saved $50 backorder bid on a domain that was never going to drop.

Rule: Only treat domains as confirmed drops AFTER the redemption period ends (typically 30-day grace + 30-day redemption = 60 days post-expiry).

Lesson 3: Afternic Nameservers = Investor Premium

Domains on ns1.afternic.com / ns2.afternic.com are aftermarket premium listings held by domain investors. They are NOT expired auction domains. Any price shown is a seller-set premium price, not a closeout or auction price.

Examples: chapter7bankruptcy.com and flashcardsonline.com both had Afternic nameservers with 4 protective status codes (clientDeleteProhibited + clientRenewProhibited + clientTransferProhibited + clientUpdateProhibited).

Rule: Check nameservers before recommending any "deal." Afternic NS = premium aftermarket.

Lesson 4: Verification Compounds

Cumulative verification savings across 4 sprints:
Sprint 9: $970 (devhub.io debunked) + Sprint 10: $225 (bid reductions) + Sprint 12: $3,655 (fake closeouts + renewal) = $4,850 total.

That's an 8x return on the $600 budget through verification alone — before buying a single new domain.

Rule: Verify everything. The cost of checking is near-zero. The cost of NOT checking can be catastrophic.
