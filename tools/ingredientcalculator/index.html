From the field notes

Things
recipes won't
tell you.

  • 3 eggs × 1.5→4½ eggs
    Round up. There is no such thing as half an egg, unless you weigh it (≈ 50 g per large).
  • 1 cup flour→120 g (sifted)
    A scooped cup can be 140 g+. Spoon and level, or weigh — your bakes will thank you.
  • ½ tsp salt→not always ½
    Diamond Crystal kosher weighs half of Morton. Read the box before doubling salty things.
  • 2× pan size→not 2× time
    Volume scales linearly; baking time doesn't. Expect 1.2–1.4× the original time, and check early.
  • leaveners→scale with care
    Doubling baking soda can make a cake taste metallic. When in doubt, leave the leavener and scale everything else.
№04 · By the numbers

A cup is
not a cup.

Volume measurements lie. The same cup holds anywhere from 90 grams of rolled oats to 340 grams of honey — almost four times the weight. This is why your grandmother's recipe never tastes the same twice, and why bakeries weigh.
