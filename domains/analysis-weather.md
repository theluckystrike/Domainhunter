**Generated:** 2026-05-01
**Total words:** 98 (all 3-6 letters, uncommon/non-premium pattern)

## Premium Avoidance Strategy

**The pattern that works:** Atmospheric/natural words that are *evocative* but NOT marketing buzzwords. The confirmed cheap words (mist, dawn, dusk, dew, soot, foam, tide at $1.54/yr) share traits:
- They describe natural phenomena, not human aspirations
- They are concrete/sensory, not abstract/aspirational
- They do NOT appear in brand taglines or ad copy regularly

**The pattern that fails:** Words that double as marketing adjectives (glow, soft, gold, pure, bold, cool, wild, wave) are premium because brands use them as descriptors.

**Key filter:** If the word could complete "Our product is ___" or "Feel ___", it is likely premium. If it describes weather, geology, or texture as a *noun*, it is likely cheap.

## Methodology

Words were selected by cross-referencing:
1. Meteorological glossaries (WMO, NOAA)
2. Geological term databases (USGS)
3. Textile/material dictionaries
4. British/archaic English vocabulary lists
5. Filtered against known premium word lists (marketing adjectives, aspiration words)

The premium filter removes any word that:
- Functions commonly as a positive adjective ("soft", "bold", "pure")
- Appears frequently in advertising copy
- Has high commercial intent in Google Ads
- Is a common brand name or product descriptor

Words that survive are primarily **concrete nouns describing natural phenomena, materials, or landforms** -- evocative enough to build a brand around, but too specific/technical/literary to appear on premium domain lists.
